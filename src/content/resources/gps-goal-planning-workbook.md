**Where dreams become destinations.**

This workbook walks through five steps. Work them in order — each one feeds the next.

## Step 1 — Reflect Back

The first step to Goal Planning Success is to pause and reflect, to think about the previous year.
This helps us understand our recent past: where did we spend our time? What worked? What didn't
work? Please don't speed through this step.

> Consider taking a long walk to "get in the zone" and deeply think about last year. Getting away
> from your daily commitments and distractions will help you focus on this task.

1. Where have I been? Where am I now? Did I accomplish all that I intended to do last year?
2. More specifically:
   - What did I accomplish?
   - Where did I go?
   - How did I feel?
   - Who did I encounter?
   - What did I learn?

**Tip:** flip back through the pages of your paper or electronic planner and calendar for the prior
year. Then re-consider the questions above. Chances are this process will trigger a memory about
the prior year that you want to record.

### Rate your year

Rate each area below on a scale of 1 to 10 — 1 is low, 5 is average, 10 is high — measured against
your own potential or opportunity:

1. Spiritual
2. Health / Physical well-being
3. Family / Spouse
4. Work / Career
5. Personal Growth
6. Social / Friends / Community
7. Financial

These seven life areas recur through the rest of the workbook.

## Step 2 — Dream Big

If we do not live and work toward our own dreams, we will wind up helping someone else live theirs.

1. What will be my legacy? What do I dream to accomplish in life?
2. What do I dream to accomplish in the coming year?
   - What will it look like to accomplish these dreams?
   - How will I feel when I accomplish them?
   - Why are these dreams important to me?
3. What problems do I want to solve?
4. What opportunities do I want to pursue?
5. Where do I want to go?
6. Who do I want to become in the coming year?

> Who you are becoming is more important than what you are accumulating.
>
> — Robin Sharma

### If you are struggling to name your dreams, check your mindset

People with a **scarcity** mindset find it hard to invest resources because they feel so deficient.
They always feel — in an expedient way — that their commodities are about to run out. If you have a
scarcity mindset, it will be difficult to escape the limitations of this way of thinking and
discover your own dreams.

People with an **abundance** mindset believe that today's short-term pain, sacrifice, and investment
in time, energy, and money will eventually bring long-term growth, blessings, and success. As you
adopt an abundance mindset, your dreams have a way of coming into focus.

John C. Maxwell talks about the gap between two significant questions:

- *"Can I?"* — a question asked by those with a scarcity mindset.
- *"How can I?"* — a question asked by those with an abundance mindset.

With that healthy, abundant mindset in mind, read back through the questions above and ask yourself,
"How can I?"

## Step 3 — Plan & Write

For each of the seven life areas, ask three questions, then write at least one goal for that area.

**A. "What do I want to accomplish?"** Structure what you write as specific, actionable and
realistic.

- **Specific** — your goals must identify exactly what you want to accomplish.
- **Action** — your goals should lead to an action verb.
- **Realistic** — you are looking for progress, not perfection. Goals should be challenging and
  should stretch you, while staying within common sense.

**B. "How will I know it happened?"** This is all about measuring.

- **Measurable** — you can't manage what you can't measure. Be specific about the results you want.
- **Celebrate** — you must know when you hit a goal so you can celebrate and reward yourself.

**C. "How many weeks or months will it take to achieve this goal?"** For this exercise, think in
big-picture terms. Concrete start and stop dates come later.

Write at least one goal for each of the seven areas: Spiritual · Health/Physical well-being ·
Family/Spouse · Work/Career · Personal Growth · Social/Friends/Community · Financial.

> **Research proves that you are 42% more likely to achieve your goals, simply by writing them
> down.**
>
> Source: Dominican University of California Goal Study, Dr. Gail Matthews PhD.

## Step 4 — Prioritize & Focus

To reach our dreams we need laser-like focus directed toward one top-priority goal. Look back at
your list from Step 3 and ask:

1. Which goal, when achieved, will impact my life the most?
2. Which goal, when accomplished, will make some of the other goals more achievable?
3. Looking back at your Reflect Back pages — does your assessment of last year highlight an area
   that needs attention sooner rather than later? Which goal represents an area that, if left
   unimproved, might threaten my dreams for the future?
4. What goal will take the **least** amount of time to achieve? Reaching this goal quickly could
   create real momentum. Mark it "MO", for **MO**mentum.

Now rank your goals in order of importance. Start with the goal marked "MO" and make it your top
priority — early success energizes you to start the next one.

Mark your priority order on the Plan & Write pages, then write your top-priority goal down. This is
your **Focus Goal**: the one you will devote your singular focus to as you start the new year.

> Concentration is the secret of strength.
>
> — Ralph Waldo Emerson

## Step 5 — Act & Track

For a someday dream to become this year's destination, you need an action plan.

1. **Focus Goal** — refer to the Focus Goal you identified in Step 4.
2. **Action ideas** — break the goal down into actions you can do on a regular basis. If your goal
   is to lose ten pounds in the first quarter, consider what physical activity you could safely do
   regularly.
3. **What** — determine how much of the action you will do. For exercise, name a distance or an
   amount of time. For a sales goal, a number of prospecting calls per day.
4. **When** — how many times a week will you perform your action, and at what time of day?
5. **Where** — where will you do this activity?

### Action plan template

> During the next week I will *(activity)* on *(day or days)* at *(time of day)* at/in *(place)*.

**Exercise example:** During the next week, I will walk three miles on Monday, Wednesday, Friday and
Saturday at 7:00am in my neighborhood.

**Sales example:** During the next week, I will make 10 prospecting calls on Monday, Wednesday and
Friday at 8:30am at my office.

Post your action plan somewhere prominent, where you will see it daily.

### Track

Track your progress toward your destination — weighing in weekly, or tallying the calls you actually
made. Tracking lets you:

1. Acknowledge small wins on the way to the final goal, and
2. Make adjustments if you discover the goal needs refining.
